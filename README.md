# INTRODUCTION

## Nigerian Farm Production and Profitability Analysis

Agriculture remains one of the most important sectors of the Nigerian economy, playing a significant role in food security, employment generation, rural development, and economic growth. With Nigeria's growing population and increasing demand for agricultural products, improving farm productivity, managing production costs, and maximizing profitability have become essential priorities for farmers, agribusinesses, investors, and policymakers.

Despite the enormous agricultural potential of Nigeria, the sector continues to face several challenges, including high production costs, inadequate infrastructure, seasonal variations, transportation difficulties, inefficient resource utilization, and differences in agricultural productivity across geographical regions. These challenges directly influence farmers' operational efficiency, revenue generation, and overall profitability.

Understanding the relationship between agricultural production, operating expenses, crop performance, and profitability is therefore essential for making informed decisions that can improve agricultural sustainability and economic outcomes.

This project, titled **"Nigerian Farm Production and Profitability Analysis,"** focuses on evaluating agricultural performance across Nigeria using data analytics and interactive visualization techniques. The analysis examines key agricultural and financial indicators to identify production patterns, assess crop profitability, compare regional performance, and uncover the major factors influencing agricultural revenue and expenditure.

The analysis was developed using **Microsoft Power BI**, providing an interactive dashboard that transforms agricultural data into meaningful insights for effective decision-making.

## Overview of the Dataset

The analysis covers approximately **5,000 farm records**, providing a broad perspective on agricultural production and financial performance across different crops, states, and geopolitical regions in Nigeria.

Based on the dashboard, the agricultural operations represented in the dataset generated approximately:

- **Total Farms Analyzed:** 5,000
- **Total Agricultural Production:** 136,315.78 tonnes
- **Total Revenue:** ₦45.86 billion
- **Total Production and Operational Costs:** ₦11.89 billion
- **Total Profit:** ₦33.97 billion
- **Overall Profit Margin:** 74.07%
- **Average Yield per Hectare:** 6.17

These indicators provide an overview of the financial performance and production capacity of the farms included in the dataset. The reported profit margin of 74.07% suggests strong overall profitability within the analyzed records, although profitability varies across crop types and geographical locations.

The dataset covers major agricultural crops, including cassava, tomato, yam, rice, maize, pepper, beans, soybean, groundnut, and sorghum. These crops contribute differently to total production, revenue generation, operating costs, and overall agricultural profitability.

## Focus of the Analysis

The analysis explores several important dimensions of agricultural performance in Nigeria.

**1. Agricultural Production Performance**

This aspect examines the total volume of agricultural products harvested across different crop categories. It identifies crops with the highest production levels and evaluates how production output differs across farming activities.

**2. Crop Profitability and Revenue Generation**

The analysis investigates the financial contribution of individual crops by comparing their revenue, production costs, and profit. This helps identify the most profitable crops and highlights opportunities for improving financial returns.

**3. Regional and State-Level Agricultural Performance**

Nigeria's six geopolitical zones have different agricultural characteristics, environmental conditions, and production capacities. This analysis compares profitability across these regions and individual states to identify areas with stronger financial performance and those with opportunities for improvement.

**4. Seasonal Agricultural Performance**

Agricultural activities in Nigeria are influenced by wet and dry seasons. The analysis evaluates seasonal differences in production and revenue to understand how agricultural performance changes throughout the farming year.

**5. Production and Operational Cost Analysis**

Profitability depends not only on revenue generation but also on effective cost management. This project examines agricultural expenses, including labour costs, transportation costs, and other operational expenditures, to understand their contributions to overall production costs.

**6. Agricultural Yield and Land Productivity**

The analysis also evaluates crop yield per hectare to provide insights into land productivity and the efficiency of agricultural output across different crop categories.

## Purpose and Significance of the Analysis

The primary purpose of this project is to transform agricultural production and financial data into actionable insights that support data-driven decision-making within Nigeria's agricultural sector.

By identifying profitable crops, evaluating regional performance, comparing seasonal productivity, and examining operational expenses, the analysis provides useful information that can assist farmers, agricultural investors, agribusiness managers, and policymakers in making more informed decisions.

Furthermore, the project demonstrates how business intelligence tools such as Power BI can be applied to agricultural data to reveal patterns, highlight performance differences, and communicate complex information through clear and interactive visualizations.

Ultimately, **the Nigerian Farm Production and Profitability Analysis aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of agricultural productivity, financial performance, and cost efficiency, while identifying opportunities for improved profitability, resource allocation, and sustainable agricultural development in Nigeria.**

## TOOLS USED FOR THE ANALYSIS

The **Nigerian Farm Production and Profitability Analysis** was developed using data analytics and business intelligence techniques to transform agricultural data into meaningful insights. The following tools can be documented as part of the analytical process, subject to confirmation that they were used.

### 1. Microsoft Excel – Data Preparation and Cleaning

Microsoft Excel can be used for the initial examination, cleaning, and preparation of the agricultural dataset before importing it into Power BI.

Key activities include:
- Identifying and removing duplicate records.
- Checking for missing values and inconsistent entries.
- Standardizing crop names, state names, and regional classifications.
- Ensuring numerical fields such as revenue, production costs, profit, and yield are correctly formatted.
- Reviewing the dataset for accuracy and consistency.

### 2. Power BI – Data Analysis and Visualization

**Microsoft Power BI** was used to develop the interactive dashboard for analyzing agricultural production, financial performance, and profitability across Nigeria.

Key activities include:
- Creating interactive dashboards to visualize agricultural performance.
- Analyzing revenue, production costs, profit, and profit margins.
- Comparing crop performance across different states and geopolitical regions.
- Evaluating seasonal production and revenue trends.
- Visualizing transportation costs, labour expenses, and crop yields.
- Presenting key performance indicators (KPIs) to support data-driven decision-making.

### 3. Power Query – Data Transformation

Power Query, available within Power BI, can be used to clean, transform, and structure raw agricultural data for analysis.

Key activities include:
- Changing column data types.
- Handling missing values and data inconsistencies.
- Renaming and organizing columns.
- Creating conditional columns where necessary.
- Preparing structured data for reporting and visualization.

### 4. DAX (Data Analysis Expressions) – Calculations and Measures

DAX is a formula language in Power BI used to create calculated measures and perform analytical computations.

Examples of measures relevant to this project include:

- **Total Revenue:** Sum of all revenue generated from agricultural activities.
- **Total Cost:** Sum of agricultural production and operational expenses.
- **Total Profit:** Total Revenue minus Total Cost.
- **Profit Margin (%):** (Total Profit / Total Revenue) × 100.
- **Total Production:** Sum of agricultural production quantities in tonnes.
- **Average Yield per Hectare:** Average of the recorded yield-per-hectare values.

These measures support comparisons of profitability, production efficiency, and financial performance across crops and regions.

### 5. Power BI Visualizations – Dashboard Presentation

Different visualization techniques were applied to communicate the findings clearly and effectively.

These include:
- **KPI Cards:** To display total farms, revenue, costs, profit, profit margin, production, and average yield.
- **Bar and Column Charts:** To compare crop profitability, production volumes, revenue, costs, and regional performance.
- **Pie Charts:** To illustrate seasonal contributions to agricultural production and revenue.
- **Interactive Navigation:** To organize the dashboard into Overview, Crop Analysis, Regional Analysis, and Farming Practices sections.

These visualizations make it easier to identify trends, compare performance, and communicate analytical findings to stakeholders.

# KEY FINDINGS AND INSIGHTS

## Nigerian Farm Production and Profitability Analysis

### 1. Overall Agricultural Performance Shows Strong Profitability

The analysis of **5,000 farm records** reveals strong overall financial performance, with total agricultural revenue reaching approximately **₦45.86 billion**, total operational costs of **₦11.89 billion**, and total profit of **₦33.97 billion**.

The agricultural operations recorded an overall **profit margin of 74.07%**, indicating that approximately ₦74 was retained as profit for every ₦100 generated in revenue, based on the costs captured in the dataset.

Additionally, total agricultural production reached approximately **136,315.78 tonnes**, with an average yield of **6.17 per hectare**, as reported in the dashboard.

**Key Insight:** The farms analyzed demonstrate strong aggregate profitability, suggesting that agricultural operations within the dataset generated substantial financial returns relative to recorded costs.

### 2. Tomato Is the Most Profitable Crop

Tomato emerged as the highest profit-generating crop, contributing approximately **₦9.07 billion** in total profit, followed by:

- Cassava: ₦6.58 billion
- Yam: ₦4.15 billion
- Pepper: ₦3.72 billion
- Rice: ₦3.69 billion

Tomato also generated the highest total revenue at approximately **₦10.59 billion**, making it the leading crop in terms of both revenue generation and profitability.

In contrast, sorghum recorded the lowest total profit at approximately **₦726.89 million**.

**Key Insight:** Tomato is the strongest contributor to overall agricultural profitability in the dataset, demonstrating that crops with high financial returns can contribute significantly to farm income even without having the highest production volume.

### 3. Cassava Dominates Agricultural Production Volume

Cassava recorded the highest production volume at approximately **47,680 tonnes**, accounting for about **35% of total agricultural output**.

Other major contributors include:

- Tomato: 25,160 tonnes
- Yam: 24,210 tonnes
- Maize: 9,770 tonnes
- Rice: 9,300 tonnes

Despite having the highest production volume, cassava ranked second in total profitability, behind tomato.

**Key Insight:** Higher production volume does not necessarily translate into higher profitability. Cassava leads in production quantity, while tomato generates greater financial returns, highlighting the importance of analyzing both production output and economic performance.

### 4. North Central Nigeria Leads in Regional Profitability

The North Central region recorded the highest total agricultural profit at approximately **₦7.9 billion**.

The regional profit distribution shows:

- North Central: ₦7.9 billion
- South West: ₦6.8 billion
- North West: ₦6.7 billion
- South South: ₦4.9 billion
- North East: ₦4.3 billion
- South East: ₦3.4 billion

The South East recorded the lowest total profit among the six geopolitical zones.

**Key Insight:** Agricultural profitability varies considerably across Nigeria's geopolitical regions. North Central contributes the highest total profit, while South East contributes the lowest, indicating differences in aggregate financial performance that warrant further investigation.

### 5. Ogun State Records the Highest Agricultural Profit

At the state level, Ogun State emerged as the leading contributor to agricultural profitability, generating approximately **₦1.48 billion**.

Other high-performing states include:

- Benue: ₦1.32 billion
- Kogi: ₦1.19 billion
- Oyo: ₦1.19 billion
- FCT: ₦1.18 billion

**Key Insight:** Ogun State demonstrates the strongest total profit contribution among the states displayed in the dashboard. This makes it an important location for further investigation into the crop mix, production scale, revenue generation, and cost structures associated with higher profitability.

### 6. Wet Season Farming Dominates Production and Revenue

The analysis reveals that wet season farming contributes a significantly larger proportion of agricultural production and revenue than dry season farming.

**Production by Season:**
- Wet Season: 68.66%
- Dry Season: 31.34%

**Revenue by Season:**
- Wet Season: ₦32.89 billion (71.71%)
- Dry Season: ₦12.97 billion (28.29%)

**Key Insight:** Wet season farming is the major contributor to agricultural output and revenue within the dataset. However, dry season farming still accounts for a meaningful share of agricultural activity, indicating its importance in supporting production outside the main rainy season.

### 7. Cassava Has the Highest Total Production Cost

Cassava recorded the highest total production cost at approximately **₦2.12 billion**, followed by:

- Yam: ₦1.54 billion
- Tomato: ₦1.53 billion
- Rice: ₦1.49 billion
- Maize: ₦1.42 billion

Although cassava has the highest production cost, it also records the largest production volume and the second-highest total profit.

**Key Insight:** High production costs do not automatically indicate poor financial performance. Cassava's substantial production expenses are accompanied by strong output and profitability, emphasizing the need to evaluate costs alongside production scale and revenue.

### 8. Labour Costs Represent a Smaller Share of Recorded Costs

The dashboard reports approximately:

- Labour Cost: ₦1.70 billion
- Other Costs: ₦3.49 billion

Within this particular cost comparison, other costs account for approximately **67.2%**, while labour accounts for **32.8%**.

**Key Insight:** Other operational expenses exceed labour costs in the displayed comparison, suggesting that non-labour expenses deserve significant attention when evaluating opportunities for cost optimization.

### 9. North Central Records the Highest Transportation Cost

Transportation expenses differ across Nigeria's geopolitical regions, with North Central recording the highest cost.

The regional breakdown includes:

- North Central: ₦465.31 million
- South West: ₦438.88 million
- North West: ₦398.75 million
- South South: ₦295.54 million
- North East: ₦240.52 million
- South East: ₦224.85 million

Interestingly, North Central also recorded the highest total agricultural profit.

**Key Insight:** North Central has both the highest transportation expenditure and the highest total profit. This suggests that high aggregate logistics expenses can coexist with strong profitability, although additional analysis is required to determine whether transportation costs are proportionately efficient.

### 10. Cassava Records the Highest Yield per Hectare

The crop yield analysis indicates that cassava recorded the highest displayed yield per hectare, followed by tomato and yam.

The results show:

- Cassava: 15
- Tomato: 13
- Yam: 11
- Pepper: 5
- Rice: 3
- Maize: 3
- Sorghum: 2
- Soybean: 2
- Groundnut: 2
- Beans: 1

**Key Insight:** Cassava demonstrates the highest land productivity based on the displayed yield measure, while tomato combines relatively high yield with the strongest total financial returns.

This highlights the importance of evaluating both land productivity and profitability when assessing agricultural performance.

## Overall Analytical Insight

The Nigerian Farm Production and Profitability Analysis reveals that agricultural performance differs significantly across crop categories, geographical regions, and farming seasons.

Tomato leads in revenue generation and profitability, while cassava dominates production volume and yield per hectare. North Central contributes the highest regional profit, Ogun State leads among the displayed states, and wet season farming accounts for the majority of agricultural production and revenue.

The analysis also shows that production costs, labour expenses, and transportation expenditures are important components of agricultural financial performance.

**Overall, the findings emphasize that agricultural success should not be measured by production volume alone, but through a combination of profitability, revenue generation, cost efficiency, seasonal performance, and land productivity.**

These insights provide a foundation for informed decisions on crop selection, agricultural investment, cost management, and resource allocation.
